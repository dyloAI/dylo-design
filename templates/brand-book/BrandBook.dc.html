THE IDEA

We make dílo,
not robota.

Robota is the old Czech word for forced, repetitive, anonymous toil — it's where the word “robot” comes from.

Dílo is its opposite: an authored, crafted, singular work — an opus, a made thing someone puts their name to.

A software factory that builds the second, never the first.

ROBOTA · forced · repetitive · anonymous → DÍLO · authored · crafted · singular

THE OPPOSITE

robota — forced labour, drudgery. Coined into “robot” by the Czech writer Karel Čapek in his 1920s play R.U.R.

Our mark is the keystone — the last, most exact stone that makes an arch stand. Not more bricks; the crafted piece the whole system holds onto.
